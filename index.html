    01

    General Introduction

    Man is born free, yet finds himself in chains everywhere — desire, body, mind, ego, and duty. Most of us live without ever fully understanding these forces or how they shape us.

    This page uses Newton's Laws as a starting point to understand self-realization on a practical, everyday level — and to ask a simple question: is fate governed by pattern and law, the way gravity is, or is it random?

    Life today runs on constant pressure, and a genuine pause has become rare. The science of self-realization — knowing your own strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats — starts with understanding your own configuration. That is where Biometrics (Palmistry, Numerology, and Face Reading) comes in: it does not predict a fixed future, it reveals the parameters you are working with, so you can live more effectively.

    Applied well, this kind of analysis can help someone see clearly what path suits them, what they are best equipped to study or pursue, and where mismatches — in career or in relationships — are likely to cause friction. Used early enough, it can help prevent some of life's more painful outcomes: failed careers, incompatible marriages, and the distress that follows.

    02

    Newton's Third Law of Gravitation

    "For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction" — this is also, almost word for word, the Law of Karma.

    As we sow, so we reap. Positive action returns reward; negative action returns consequence. Everyday life bears this out constantly. Three questions follow naturally from Newton's law:

    1. What allowed Newton to see this law in an ordinary event?
    Two qualifications: emotional (the right disposition to notice and pursue the insight) and logical (a thorough grasp of natural law). Every achievement — from a child's first school admission to an engineer clearing a competitive exam — requires the right qualification first.

    2. What does this say about human parameters?
    This leads into the idea of "MS Office" — covered in the next section.

    3. Who made nature and its laws?
    Covered under IPR, God and Prayer, further below.

    Every person operates two "offices" at once:

    • Material Office — Body, Mind, Intelligence, Ego (the gross aspect — visible, physically sensed)
    • Spiritual Office — Soul & Super Soul (the subtle aspect — invisible, only felt through action)

    Our parents give us our body — the hardware. But they cannot tell us what our mind, intelligence, or soul will become over time; that information simply is not theirs to give. We learn who we are only through lived experience.

    Even physically, every cell in the body is replaced roughly every seven years — yet we remain recognizably "the same person." That continuity comes from mind, intelligence, and ego, not from the body itself. And when someone dies, we describe it as the person having left the body — an acknowledgment, even in ordinary language, that the person was always more than the physical form.

    In short: our understanding of ourselves — especially the mind, intelligence, and soul — is still remarkably incomplete, even with all of modern science's progress on the body.

    04

    Human Life Cycle

    Bi-GOD: Birth, Growth, Old Age, Death

    Human Life Cycle — Bi GOD
    Birth - Growth - Old Age - Death

    None of us controls these four events.

    • We do not choose our parents, our birth date, or the timing of our own conception.
    • Growth has a natural ceiling — after a point, decline is inevitable.
    • We cannot stop disease outright, only try to prevent or treat it.
    • We cannot stop ageing.
    • We cannot defy death, or predict exactly when it will arrive.

    This absence of control across every stage of life is itself evidence: something beyond individual will is directing the larger pattern.

    Who authored these laws? As per Bhagavad Gita As It Is, God is understood as the Supreme Person — Generator, Operator, Destroyer. Even scientific voices have pointed the same direction: physicist and chemist Dr. John C. Cotran (former Chairman, Science & Mathematics, University of Minnesota) argued that matter is not eternal, must have had a beginning, and that a beginning implies an agent with the intelligence and will to create and sustain it — pointing toward a creator possessing omniscience, omnipotence, and omnipresence.

    Time, Action & Reaction Chart

    Time itself is infinite; our presence in it is finite, moving through past, present, and future. Every present reaction — including our own birth — traces back to a past action. That is Newton's law again, applied on both the emotional (inherent nature) and logical (parentage, ability) fronts.

    The logical side can be traced and understood to some extent. The emotional side largely cannot — it can only be graded loosely as excess, normal, or deficient. In the Material Office, action and reaction repeat within visible time cycles. In the Spiritual Office, they do not repeat in the same way — they take the shape of fate itself, across past, present, and future.

    The present age (Kali Yuga) has macro and micro features, per the Bhagavad Gita:

    • Macro — defined by quarrel and hypocrisy; a vast time-cycle, of which we are roughly 5,000+ years in.
    • Micro — each day divides into three 8-hour blocks: Goodness, Passion, and Ignorance — the material modes of nature that shape our thousands of daily decisions.

    Intellectual Property Rights (IPR), GOD and Prayer

    We are used to paying for value received — that is how IPR works for human effort. Consider a computer: every time it boots, it silently runs an integrity check on its own memory and systems before letting you use it. We never think to thank the system for that.

    Now consider ourselves: every morning we wake up and simply function — heart beating, digestion running, mind engaging — without ever running our own "integrity check." Someone is doing that maintenance for us, continuously, without asking to be paid.

    That someone is God. Prayer is how we acknowledge and pay that debt. Offering it is a positive action; withholding it is a negative one — and we are free to choose either.

    Positive action is rewarded — materially and otherwise — with beauty, wealth, power, fame, knowledge, and ultimately the highest stage: renunciation. Negative action brings the corresponding deficiency.

    There is supporting science here too: research shows that meditation and prayer measurably coordinate electrical activity between the brain's hemispheres, increasing mental plasticity — literally changing how we perceive the world.

    09

    Conclusion

    Yes — fate is scientific.

    Biometrics offers empirical, evidence-based insight into a person's path from birth to death. It functions like advanced probability (existence and death being, in a sense, a 50/50 proposition) and like advanced calculus — accounting for constantly shifting variables of time, intelligence, and emotion, no two days ever identical.

    And yet, despite all that constant change, the underlying pattern — birth, growth, old age, death — proceeds regardless. Understanding your own configuration through biometric analysis does not remove that pattern, but it does let you walk your path more clearly, and with the chance of a more graceful outcome at every stage.
